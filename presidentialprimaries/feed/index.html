]]> By: Betsy https://righteousmind.com/presidentialprimaries/#comment-1326 Tue, 29 Mar 2016 22:33:20 +0000 http://righteousmind.com/?p=2057#comment-1326 I was hoping you could address some questions regarding two key findings from your research:

1. Trump supporters were not extreme on any of the foundations – Is it true that Trump supporters are not moral extremists, or is the pattern of data an artifact of Trump’s large share of the vote? It appears that the average American’s emphasis on each moral foundation was defined as the average rating across these 2,000 poll respondents. As such, supporters of candidates with larger shares of the vote will appear closer to the average. Indeed, Clinton and Trump supporters–the candidates with the two largest shares of the vote at 27% and 20%, respectively–appear non-extreme relative to many of the other candidates with smaller vote shares (e.g., Cruz with 9% of the vote). Do you have data that indicate national averages from a source other than this poll that could serve as a more convincing baseline? In addition to large vote share, a second way in which a candidate’s supporters could appear average in the aggregate is to comprise moral extremists who place equal and opposite emphasis on a given moral foundation. Can you provide any insight into the distribution of Trump supporters’ data to help convince us that his supporters are moral moderates and not a collection of equal and opposite extremists?

2. Moral Foundations predicted candidate preference above and beyond demographics and partisanship – This appears to be true for some candidates based on individual LOGIT models, but were the model fits compared? That is, for each candidate, is the demographics + partisanship + moral foundations model (model 1c) a better fit than the demographics + partisanship model (model 1a)? An additional LOGIT analysis that could prove valuable is one predicting Sanders versus Clinton support. For those individuals who support either Sanders or Clinton, I question whether the moral foundations improve model fit relative to a model with just demographics and partisanship as predictors. If not, such evidence would contradict the primary takeaway from this research, at least among Democrats’ supporters.

Thank you!
