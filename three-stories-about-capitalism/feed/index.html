The main point is to recognize the truth in each narrative, and to let go of any bias that one might have that leads to embracing one or the other. That is apparently difficult. Given that capitalism is winning, we must look at where it leads in the future, not just at what the past brought. One must look at the extreme power and money consolidation of our present global capitalism and critique what this does over time to distribution, to innovation, to competition and to human existence. Given that profit is the one value of capitalism, it seems clear to me where this train is going. There is nothing that demands that eventually the top will have it all and abuse the majority remaining. That appears to be the end game and this leaves little room for innovation or morality. Monopolies steal the chance of competition, free markets, creativity, or a decent living. It destroys families and communities. While capitalism has raised the standard of life globally at this juncture, it could be considered the unintended consequence on the way to monopolization and abuse. Given the fact that the balance between the owners and those that labor has been changed by globalization, abuse is there for the taking. History does not provide the road map. We are already seeing abuse all around us. But history does show us that when few have power, abuse happens and push back is difficult and generally leads to serious violence. If one believes society should concern themselves with quality of life for the majority, then this extreme consolidation of power and wealth will certainly not get us there. As you noted, the squeeze for profits is a real part of capitalism and it comes at the cost of lives. I agree there is a third story, but let’s be realistic about what that should look like and not pretend that the citizens have more power than they do. My view is that the third system should include a way for many to compete and a way to keep money and power out of the hands of so few. That system raises the quality of life over the long haul and provides hope to the many. Let’s not give up.

]]> By: Slee https://righteousmind.com/three-stories-about-capitalism/#comment-1632 Sun, 14 Jan 2018 02:09:29 +0000 http://righteousmind.com/?p=1388#comment-1632 There is one problem with story 3. The statement ‘The long compression of income inequality, which has begun in the 1930s in many western nations, ended.’.

It didn’t just end. There was a cause. That cause in the U.S. was, as far as I can tell, the Clinton tax hike in 1993. The result of the hike on income taxes was that company management compensation moved from salary to bonuses, based largely on stock options. A C.E.O. could get a raise and he taxed at 39.5%, or he/she could get stock options which would be taxed at the capital gains rate of 28%. Which would you prefer?

That is a no-brainer.

So the tax law lead to a clear benefit in changing compensation packages towards options which, with the tech boom, led to massive accumulation of wealth by those compensated with stock. It also led to a larger focus on stock performance instead of other company fundamentals.

The tax hike that preceded this was put in place, of course, to make the rich ‘pay their fair share’.

The vast increase income inequality wasn’t an issue with capitalism but rather a specific policy enacted, one would suspect*, with the goal of enabling the politicians to talk about how they were making the rich pay.

That is a political issue in a ‘Be careful what you wish for’ way.

Slee
